  <context>
CONTEXT.md D-16 delegates the precise shapes to the planner but fixes the constraints: JSONB stays
JSONB, the shape is defined and validated in `packages/api-contracts/`, and validation runs in the
sync push path. The columns already exist and are untyped (`apps/api/src/db/schema/equipment.ts`),

  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="option-a">
      <name>Recommended — objects for all three columns, counts in pairs, machines identified by a stable client UUID</name>
      <pros>
`available_plates` as `{ weightKg, pairCount }[]` states the unit of counting explicitly, which is
where a bare `count` would be ambiguous (plates or pairs?) and would silently halve or double every
breakdown. `dumbbell_increments_kg` as `{ weightKg }[]` leaves room for a per-weight count later
without a shape change. `machine_availability` as
`{ id, name, equipmentType, available, stackMinKg, stackMaxKg, stackIncrementKg, baseResistanceKg }[]`
gives GYM-03 all three of its named fields plus a stable `id` that the session-unavailable
reference and the Gym Profile Editor can both key on across renames. The session value is
`{ kind: 'equipment_type' | 'machine' | 'dumbbell', ... }[]`, matching exactly the three things
UI-SPEC says can be marked unavailable. Every decimal is an exact string.
      </pros>
      <cons>
More verbose than bare arrays of numbers; every field needs a validator branch.
      </cons>
    </option>
    <option id="option-b">
      <name>Flat arrays of decimal strings, counts implied by repetition</name>
      <pros>Trivial to validate and serialize.</pros>
      <cons>
Cannot express a machine's stack range or base resistance at all, so GYM-03 would need a fourth
column or an out-of-band encoding; repetition as a count makes a 6-pair 20 kg entry six identical
strings, which the editor's stepper cannot round-trip cleanly.
      </cons>
    </option>
  </options>
